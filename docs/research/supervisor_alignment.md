## Confirmed Facts from the Discussion

- Two connected questions were positively received: whether uncertainty can be
  decomposed by source, and whether the resulting signals can improve
  calibration and useful decisions.
- Explaining where uncertainty comes from was emphasized alongside quantifying
  it correctly.
- Concrete situations in which overconfidence causes a problem were requested.
- Healthcare was viewed as a strong motivating domain, while a theoretical or
  other decision-making route was not ruled out.
- Distributional image--text embeddings were considered worth investigating,
  particularly as a possible way to distinguish ambiguity from conflict.
- The requested initial intellectual exercise was: why the idea is useful, how
  it could be achieved, and what prior work already exists.

## Inferences Used for Planning

- The strongest first paper is unlikely to be “apply a Gaussian embedding” or
  “use Wasserstein distance.” A stronger novelty claim requires conditional
  source identification and matched evidence beyond simpler predictors.
- Controlled intervention and construct validity should precede calibration
  and selective-decision claims.
- Medical image--text evidence can supply a consequential use case, but the
  initial task should be narrower than autonomous diagnosis or open-ended
  report generation.

These are strategic inferences, not statements attributed verbatim to a
supervisor.

## Researcher Decisions

- Use **Ambiguity Is Not Conflict** as the standalone project identity.
- Pursue exactly one primary route centred on controlled cross-modal conflict
  identification.
- Plan for a NeurIPS 2027 Main Track submission, subject to scientific kill
  gates and the official 2027 call.
- Use medical image--text systems as the primary validation domain unless a
  formal decision record changes the route.
